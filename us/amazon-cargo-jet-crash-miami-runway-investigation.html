Federal Investigators Probe Amazon Cargo Jet’s Fiery Runway Crash That Killed 5 in Miami

Federal aviation safety officials have launched an urgent multi-agency investigation after an Amazon Air cargo jet suffered a catastrophic runway landing and erupted into flames at Miami International Airport, killing five people and shutting down airfield operations.

Christopher Reed
| Senior Energy & Transportation Correspondent • Sept. 08, 2026
SHARE

F ederal aviation safety regulators have opened a full-scale forensic inquiry after a twin-engine Boeing 767 cargo aircraft operating on behalf of Amazon Air suffered a devastating runway accident, skidded into airport ground facilities, and burst into an uncontrollable fireball at Miami International Airport early Tuesday, claiming five lives.

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) dispatched an elite Go-Team of structural, avionics, and powerplant specialists to South Florida following the pre-dawn disaster. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) confirmed that the flight was inbound from an East Coast freight sorting hub when the flight crew declared an emergency while on final approach to Runway 9, reporting severe hydraulic anomalies and uncontrollable pitch oscillation moments before touching down.

Catastrophic Landing and Immediate Emergency Response in Miami

Eyewitnesses and airport surveillance footage revealed the wide-body freighter contacting the asphalt at an unusually high rate of descent. Seconds after main gear contact, the port-side landing gear collapsed, causing the wing to shear against the runway surface. Thousands of gallons of jet aviation fuel instantly ignited into a massive blaze that engulfed the fuselage and tore through nearby ground support equipment before the burning airframe came to rest in the grass overrun area.

More than thirty firefighting rigs from Miami-Dade Fire Rescue's Airport Division responded within ninety seconds, laying down vast blankets of specialized chemical foam to contain the inferno. Despite their rapid arrival, local authorities confirmed that three crew members in the cockpit and two ground maintenance workers stationed in an adjacent taxiway shelter succumbed to blunt-force trauma and intense thermal conditions.

NTSB and FAA Launch Go-Team Investigation into Flight Recorders

At a preliminary press conference outside Miami-Dade Aviation Department headquarters, NTSB lead investigator Marcus Vance confirmed that search teams had successfully recovered both the Cockpit Voice Recorder (CVR) and the Digital Flight Data Recorder (FDR) from the charred empennage of the wreckage. Both "black boxes" have been transported under federal chain of custody to the NTSB's vehicle recording laboratory in Washington, D.C., for forensic readout and acoustic analysis.

"Our mission is not merely to determine what occurred during those final fatal ninety seconds, but to uncover every systemic factor that contributed to this loss of life," Vance stated. "We will be examining flight control telemetry, maintenance logs, hydraulic line pressures, runway surface conditions, and crew duty cycles to build an unassailable factual reconstruction."

Amazon Air and Fleet Operator Scrutinized Over Maintenance Logs

The tragedy represents one of the deadliest accidents involving dedicated e-commerce air freight in recent American aviation history. The aircraft, operated under a long-term wet-lease arrangement by Atlas Air Worldwide, had accumulated over twenty-two thousand flight cycles. In a statement released Tuesday morning, Amazon expressed profound grief, pledging total transparency and cooperation with federal investigators while deploying employee assistance counselors to the victims' families.

Industry regulators are expected to review recent maintenance work performed on the aircraft's landing gear actuators and hydraulic redundant valves. Federal safety records indicate that cargo conversions of passenger Boeing 767s have long served as workhorses for express package delivery, but the rapid expansion of next-day logistics networks has placed unprecedented utilization demands on aging converted freighters across major domestic logistics corridors.

Aviation Safety Experts Highlight Pressures in Overnight Air Freight

Aviation labor unions and flight safety researchers have pointed to growing systemic pressures within the overnight cargo sector, where tight turnaround schedules, heavy nighttime operations, and outsourced maintenance agreements can compound technical vulnerabilities. The International Brotherhood of Teamsters, which represents cargo pilots at major carriers, issued a call for heightened oversight of contract freight operations and mandatory safety stand-downs across comparable airframes.

Miami International Airport reopened two non-affected commercial runways by late afternoon following extensive safety sweeps and debris clearance, although dozens of passenger flights experienced cascading delays. As federal investigators begin their year-long inquiry into the Miami crash, lawmakers on the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee signaled plans to hold oversight hearings examining cargo fleet age and maintenance standards across the booming global logistics industry.
