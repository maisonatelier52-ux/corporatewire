Amazon Announces 16,000 Layoffs as Part of Major Restructuring Focused on AI and Cloud Services

Amazon announces 16,000 layoffs as part of a major restructuring focused on AI and cloud efficiency. The move signals a shift in the tech giant's long-term operational strategy.

Matthew Collins | Senior Technology & Global Economy Editor • Feb. 17, 2026
SHARE

A mazon said on January 28 that it would fire off 16,000 corporate workers as part of a restructuring plan to "reduce layers and remove bureaucracy. “The layoffs will affect both Amazon Web Services (AWS) and the company's retail divisions. These include teams working on Bedrock, an AI cloud service, Redshift, a data warehousing platform, and the Prime subscription service. This is the second big layoff at Amazon in three months, after the company laid off 14,000 people in October.

Affected workers in the US, UK, and India were told they needed immediate badge access limits and security help to leave Amazon locations. Employees in the U.S. will be paid in full for 90 days, and if they are not given new jobs, they will get severance pay and help finding a new job. According to internal FAQs, affected staff must return their laptops and get their personal items back by February 13. Any items that are not claimed will be given away or thrown away.

Why This News Matters:

As part of its restructuring plan, Amazon is laying off 16,000 workers. This shows that the company is becoming more efficient and focusing on AI advancements. Some divisions, like Amazon Web Services (AWS) and retail, have been affected by layoffs. However, the company has stressed that these changes are necessary to stay competitive, especially in cloud computing and artificial intelligence. The cuts come at a time when Microsoft, Google, and Meta are all trying to become the best at AI, which is making it hard for Amazon to compete. These layoffs are hard, but Amazon is making smart investments in areas that will help it grow in the future, like AI and cloud services. This shows that the company is changing in response to how technology is changing.

Amazon’s Focus on AI and Efficiency Amid Layoffs

Beth Galetti, Amazon's head of HR, says that the layoffs are part of the company's goal to become the "world's largest startup." CEO Andy Jassy has been pushing for this. Amazon will be able to work faster and take on more work because of the layoffs. The memo from Jassy said that most of the job losses were caused by improvements in AI. It said that AI automation would create new jobs and make some jobs unnecessary.

Amazon is working on making its AI and technology better because Microsoft, Google, and Meta are all trying to be the best at AI, which means Amazon has more competition. Some Amazon workers have been let go, but the company says it will keep hiring in key areas that will help it grow in the future. AI and cloud computing are two of these areas.

Amazon is closing its grocery stores, Amazon Fresh and Amazon Go, as part of its restructuring. Some of them will turn into Whole Foods Market stores. By 2026, the company wants to spend more money on its Whole Foods stores and offer same-day grocery delivery to more places. Amazon is changing its plan to improve its grocery business, and it's also investing in other important areas like AI and cloud services.

Bezos and AI Ventures Outside Amazon

Jeff Bezos, who started Amazon, is now working on other businesses, mostly ones that use AI. Bezos is now in charge of Project Prometheus, an AI company that has $6.2 billion in funding. This shows how much he wants to improve AI. They say the company is one of the best-funded AI startups, along with OpenAI, Microsoft, and Google, all of which are putting more money into AI.

Andy Jassy has said that AI will change how Amazon workers do their jobs. He says that automation powered by AI will make some jobs need fewer people to do them. Jassy told employees in a note that Generative AI would change the way work is done. This might mean that some places will have fewer jobs and others will need new ones more. Jassy's comments show that AI is becoming less important in deciding who will work at Amazon in the future and where the company stands in the global race to be the best at AI.

Amazon's Shift in Strategy and Workforce Adjustments

According to Vanguard's most recent study, AI doesn't take away as many jobs as people used to think it did. The poll shows that jobs that AI could do are growing faster than jobs in other areas. AI has made it easier for people to lose their jobs, especially those who do entry-level work that AI can do or do better. Amazon is still spending a lot of money on AI, and it might work even better if the company could find a way to make things go more smoothly. Even though the company is doing everything it can to keep up with the tech needs of the future, people are still worried about losing their jobs.

Amazon said that the layoffs were not only to save money, but also to make the company run better. This fits with its main goal, which is to stay up to date with the latest technology. Jassy also said that Amazon's AI technologies aren't unique and will likely have a big impact on many things. Even though the economy is shaky and the company is moving to AI, Amazon is still working to improve the customer experience and figure out what changes it needs to make to stay ahead of the competition.
