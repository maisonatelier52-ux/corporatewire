US Business Growth Cools in November as GDP and PCE Data Loom

US business activity slows slightly in November's flash PMI, and a Thanksgiving-week data rush, including GDP and PCE inflation, will shape Fed rate-hike bets for December.

A short week with some very big numbers

The US economy heads into Thanksgiving with a lot still to prove. Business surveys show growth is still healthy but cooling slightly, inflation remains well above the Federal Reserve's 2% goal, and the central bank has signalled it may keep raising interest rates. Before markets close for the holiday on Thursday, a crowded run of reports will show how fast the economy grew over the summer and how quickly prices are still rising. Here is a plain-English guide to the latest US economy news and what to watch for.

Business Activity Cools but Stays Strong in November

On Monday, November 23, S&P Global's flash purchasing managers' index (PMI), an early reading on business activity, showed the US composite index slipping to 54.6 in November from 55.9 in October. Any reading above 50 signals growth, so the economy is still expanding at a healthy pace. The services sector, which makes up most of the economy, eased to 54.7 from 56.1 and was below the 55.6 economists expected. Manufacturing slowed slightly to 54.5 from 54.9, in line with forecasts. The data suggest that the economy was holding up in November, even as stocks swung sharply and borrowing costs stayed high. A separate measure, the Chicago Fed National Activity Index, edged down to -0.06 in October from 0.05 in September, according to one report.

What Inflation Is Doing and What the Fed Has Said

The big question is still inflation. The Fed held its policy rate at 3.75% to 4% on October 28 by a 10-2 vote, and the minutes showed that almost all officials judged further rises would likely be appropriate. New York Fed President John Williams said earlier this month, inflation is persistent, and that the Fed still has work to do. Governor Lisa Cook said on November 20 that additional and larger rate increases may be required later to catch up. Futures markets are pricing about a 92% chance of a quarter-point hike at the December 8 to 9 meeting. For households, that means mortgage, car loan and credit card rates are unlikely to fall soon.

Thanksgiving Week Brings GDP, PCE and More

The most important reports land on Wednesday, November 25, because Thursday is a holiday. The government will publish its second estimate of third-quarter GDP, with economists expecting growth of about 3.4%, up from 1.5% in the previous quarter, although forecasts vary slightly between sources. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the PCE price index for October, is also due. Economists expect it to show prices up 3.8% from a year earlier, compared with 3.6% in September, and core PCE, which strips out food and energy, up 3.2% from 3.1%. The same morning brings personal income and spending, durable goods orders and new home sales, and the Fed's Beige Book. Earlier, on Tuesday, the Conference Board's consumer confidence index and the S&P/Case-Shiller home price index are due, with prices forecast to be up about 2.7% from a year ago.

What to Watch Next

Keep an eye on three things. First, whether the GDP and PCE numbers match forecasts, since a hotter inflation reading would add weight to the case for another Fed hike. Second, consumer spending as the holiday shopping season begins, with Black Friday on November 27. Third, the Fed's December 8 to 9 meeting and any more official comments before it. Markets close early on Friday, so the final data of the week, the October trade balance, will arrive in a thin trading session. Check the latest figures before you publish.
