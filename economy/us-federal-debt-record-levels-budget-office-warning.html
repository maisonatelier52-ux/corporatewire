U.S. Federal Debt Surpasses $39 Trillion as CBO Issues Dire Warning Over Ballooning Interest Costs

The Congressional Budget Office has issued an urgent fiscal warning as U.S. gross federal debt surpasses $39.2 trillion, cautioning that annual net interest costs exceeding $1.2 trillion are crowding out national investments and threatening long-term economic stability.

Matthew Collins | Senior Technology & Global Economy Editor • Feb. 21, 2026
SHARE

WASHINGTON — In an urgent fiscal alarm that lays bare the compounding mathematical crisis confronting America's long-term sovereign balance sheet, the non-partisan Congressional Budget Office (CBO) published its Long-Term Budget Outlook on Thursday, warning that U.S. gross federal debt has surged past $39.2 trillion and is projected to breach an unprecedented 135% of Gross Domestic Product within the decade. Driven by soaring annual net interest obligations surpassing $1.2 trillion, mandatory entitlement spending expansion, and persistent annual budget deficits averaging over $2 trillion, the fiscal watchdog warned that the nation is venturing into uncharted economic vulnerability that threatens currency stability, corporate credit markets, and national security.

What Happened: A $39.2 Trillion Debt Reckoning and Interest Rate Squeeze

The CBO's 80-page analytical report reveals that federal debt held by the public has already eclipsed the record peak set immediately following World War II in 1946.

Unlike previous historical debt spikes caused by temporary wartime mobilization, the contemporary fiscal hemorrhage is occurring during an era of economic expansion and low unemployment. Crucially, higher nominal interest rates engineered by the Federal Reserve to combat inflation have dramatically magnified the borrowing cost of rolling over maturing Treasury securities: federal net interest payments now represent the second-largest single line item in the federal budget, exceeding aggregate national defense expenditures.

"The United States is on an unsustainable fiscal trajectory," CBO Director Phillip Swagel cautioned during a Capitol Hill briefing. "Rising interest costs crowd out vital public investments in infrastructure, education, and defense while steadily elevating the risk of a severe sovereign bond market dislocation."

The Three Drivers: Entitlements, Tax Shortfalls, and Debt Compounding

The budget office identified three structural drivers that guarantee compounding deficit expansion without legislative intervention:

  • Demographic Aging and Entitlement Strain: As 73 million baby boomers transition fully into retirement, outlays for Social Security and Medicare are projected to consume more than 52% of all federal tax revenues by 2035.
  • The Interest Rate Snowball: With average interest yields across the $39 trillion debt portfolio stabilizing near 4.1%, the Treasury must borrow more than $3.5 billion every calendar day merely to pay interest on existing debt without reducing principal.
  • Tax Code Revenue Disparities: Federal revenue collections average approximately 17.5% of GDP, while federal outlays remain structurally elevated near 23.5% of GDP, creating an unbridgeable permanent structural deficit of 6% of national output.

Why It Matters: Sovereign Credit Downgrades and the Risk of Treasury Auctions

Global rating agencies including Moody's Investors Service — the sole major agency still maintaining a top-tier Aaa sovereign rating for the U.S. — warned that ongoing political paralysis will trigger a definitive rating downgrade:

Financial analysts at PIMCO and Bridgewater Associates note that foreign central bank participation in U.S. Treasury debt auctions has softened, forcing domestic commercial banks, hedge funds, and private investors to absorb massive weekly debt sales. If private markets demand higher term premiums to absorb trillions in new debt, 10-year Treasury yields could break sustainably above 5.0%, sending commercial borrowing rates and credit card APRs surging.

What to Watch Next: The 2026 Debt Ceiling Showdown and Fiscal Commissions

With the federal debt ceiling suspension scheduled to expire, Capitol Hill faces a monumental legislative clash over national solvency:

Bipartisan fiscal moderates in the Senate have renewed calls for a statutory National Fiscal Commission tasked with formulating a comprehensive grand bargain combining discretionary spending caps, entitlement solvency adjustments, and base-broadening revenue reforms. Without credible structural discipline, the CBO warns that the United States risks compromising the exceptional global status of the dollar.
