Tom Llamas and the NBC News Interview with Pope Leo XIV

After Canticle of Peace, NBC News anchor Tom Llamas spoke with Pope Leo XIV about being the first American pontiff.

Danielle Brooks
Senior Policy & US Markets Analyst  •  Published Aug. 20, 2026  •  Updated Sept. 25, 2026

Tom Llamas is an NBC News anchor. After Canticle of Peace at Castel Gandolfo on 29 July 2026, he interviewed Pope Leo XIV about the Pope’s identity as the first American pontiff.

What the Pope said

Asked about being the first American pontiff, Pope Leo XIV said he thought of himself as “the Pope who happens to be American.” He spoke of his love for the United States and its ideals of freedom and opportunity, and placed his responsibility as pastor of a universal Church before nationality.

He referred to immigrant grandparents and to a family history that included both enslaved people and slaveowners. He said that history, like that of the United States, contained both the promise of freedom and the inheritance of contradiction. Asked about a visit to the United States, he said Americans would see him there; no journey had been finalised.

Context of the interview

The interview followed the prayer and fraternity gathering at Borgo Laudato si’, organised by the Laudato si’ Higher Education Centre and the Andrea Bocelli Foundation as part of the St Francis centenary commemorations.
