A big weekend for movies, music and streaming

It has been a busy few days in American entertainment. A holiday-season movie starring Johnny Depp topped the weekend box office, a new legal fight has opened up over one of the biggest media mergers in years, and the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame welcomed its latest class in Los Angeles. There's also a fresh batch of shows landing on Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+ and Paramount+ this week. If you're trying to keep up with the latest US entertainment news, here's a plain-English look at what happened, why it matters and what to watch next.

Ebenezer Opens at No. 1 With $19 Million

Paramount's retelling of Charles Dickens' A Christmas Carol, called Ebenezer and starring Johnny Depp, opened in first place with an estimated $19 million in North America, according to industry estimates released Sunday. It earned about $6.7 million on Friday from 3,535 theaters, and it beat the family hit The Cat in the Hat, which took in $11.4 million in its second weekend. Godzilla Minus Zero was third with $8.3 million, followed by Ramayana: Rise of a Legend with $5.5 million and You Can See Everything with $5.4 million in its fifth weekend. Two other new releases landed lower down the chart. Ghost Soldier opened in sixth place with $5.2 million, and the crime comedy How to Rob a Bank took seventh with $5.1 million, a result MyNewsLA described as disappointing. The whole top of the chart added up to roughly $86.8 million for the three-day weekend.

Why This Opening Matters for Johnny Depp

The result is being read as a test of whether Depp can still draw a crowd on his own. Reports describe Ebenezer as his first film to open at No. 1 in about eight years, and it is being sold as a comeback after a long stretch away from big wide releases. A $19 million start is solid rather than spectacular, but it is a good sign for a movie that is aiming at the holiday season, when films often keep earning for weeks as audiences return for seasonal favorites. Just Jared reported that the film also debuted at No. 1 worldwide with about $42 million globally. Box office analysts will now watch how well it holds up in its second weekend, which is usually the better clue to whether word of mouth is strong. The wider market is healthy, too. The year-to-date domestic box office stands at about $8.77 billion, up around 20.7% from the same point last year.

Consumers Refile a Lawsuit Against the Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery Merger

Away from the multiplex, a legal fight is brewing over who controls so much of Hollywood. A group of consumers has refiled its antitrust lawsuit in California federal court, seeking to reverse the roughly $110 billion merger of Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery, as well as the earlier $8 billion combination of Paramount and Skydance. The same judge, U.S. District Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin, dismissed the earlier version in August but allowed an amended complaint. The plaintiffs argue that joining Paramount+ and HBO Max could mean fewer choices and higher prices, and that the companies' large debt, around $80 billion, creates pressure to raise subscription fees and cut costs. They also point to reported departures of DC Studios leaders James Gunn and Peter Safran. These are allegations and have not been established by a court. The deal closed in October after settlements with 12 state attorneys general and the Writers Guild of America. The companies have said they plan to find at least $6 billion in savings over three years, and a combined subscription bundle is planned for 2027.

What the Merger Means for Streaming Subscribers

For ordinary viewers, the practical question is what happens to their bills. Paramount+ had about 82.2 million subscribers in the third quarter, while HBO Max and HBO together had about 120.8 million, which adds up to around 203 million. The company has said it will bring technology together in the first year, offer a bundle in 2027 and eventually move to a single platform. Supporters of mergers like this usually say that scale helps a company compete with giants such as Netflix and Disney, and that savings can be spent on programming. Critics counter that fewer rivals means less pressure to keep prices down. Nothing is changing for subscribers today, but it's worth keeping an eye on any price announcements, since the lawsuit specifically says earlier Paramount+ price increases hurt consumers

Oasis, Phil Collins and Wu-Tang Clan Honored at the Rock Hall

On Saturday night, the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame held its 2026 induction ceremony at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles. The performer class includes Phil Collins, Billy Idol, Iron Maiden, Joy Division and New Order, Oasis, Sade, Luther Vandross and Wu-Tang Clan. The Early Influence Award went to Celia Cruz, Fela Kuti, Queen Latifah, MC Lyte and Gram Parsons, while the Musical Excellence Award honored Linda Creed, Arif Mardin, Jimmy Miller and Rick Rubin. Ed Sullivan received the Ahmet Ertegun Award. Fans who missed the night don't have long to wait, because the ceremony is set to air in December on ABC and Disney+. The ceremony will move back to Cleveland in 2027.

New Shows to Watch This Week

There's plenty to stream before the Thanksgiving rush. Netflix is releasing Win the Mall, a reality competition with ten teams, on Wednesday, followed on Thursday by The Altruists, an eight-episode drama about the collapse of FTX starring Anthony Boyle and Julia Garner. On Friday, Prime Video launches the two-part docuseries Melania, The Series, and Netflix adds the Korean crime thriller The Blue Road. Paramount+ has the true-crime documentary He Said, She Said: Till Death Do Us Part, Disney+ premieres the Dutch period drama Vienna Game, and PBS airs Lynn Novick's four-part documentary Crime and Punishment in America starting Monday night. HBO Max rounds out the week on Sunday with Oobah: Scams for a Better Tomorrow, a docu-comedy about elaborate hoaxes.

What to Watch Next in US Entertainment

Keep an eye on Ebenezer's second weekend, which will show whether Depp's comeback has legs heading into the holiday season. Watch for the next court filings in the Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery case, and for any word on subscription prices or the planned 2027 bundle. And if you want the full Rock Hall experience, mark your calendar for the December broadcast on ABC and Disney+. With strong ticket sales this year and a packed streaming calendar, the next few weeks should be busy for fans and for the industry.
