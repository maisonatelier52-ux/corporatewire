UK’s Starmer Seeks Greater Powers to Regulate Online Access

British Prime Minister Keir Starmer is pushing for expanded regulatory powers over digital platforms to address concerns regarding child safety, misinformation, and online harms.

Amanda Whitaker | Senior Financial & Healthcare Correspondent • Feb. 20, 2026
SHARE

British Prime Minister Keir Starmer is seeking expanded powers to regulate online access, signaling a tougher approach to digital platforms amid growing concerns over child safety, misinformation, and harmful content. The move marks a significant escalation in the UK government’s efforts to exert greater oversight over the internet and could reshape how technology companies operate in one of Europe’s largest digital markets.

Starmer’s push builds on existing legislation but goes further in proposing stronger enforcement tools, faster intervention mechanisms, and broader authority for regulators. Government officials argue that current laws have not kept pace with the scale and speed of online harms, particularly as artificial intelligence, encrypted messaging, and algorithm-driven content amplify risks. Critics, however, warn that expanding state power over online access could raise questions about privacy, free expression, and the boundaries of government oversight in a democratic society.

A tougher stance on digital harms

At the heart of Starmer’s proposal is the belief that voluntary compliance by technology firms is no longer sufficient. While existing frameworks require platforms to address illegal and harmful content, ministers say enforcement has been uneven and penalties too slow to deter misconduct.

Under the new approach, regulators would be granted greater authority to compel companies to act quickly when harmful material is identified. This could include directing platforms to restrict access to certain features, impose stricter age-verification systems, or adjust recommendation algorithms that promote harmful or misleading content.

Government sources say the aim is not to censor lawful speech, but to ensure that online spaces meet the same safety expectations as offline environments. “The digital world should not be a lawless one,” a senior official said, emphasizing that platforms must take responsibility for the risks created by their technology.

Focus on protecting children

Child safety has emerged as a central justification for expanding regulatory powers. Officials point to evidence linking excessive screen time, exposure to harmful content, and addictive design features to mental health challenges among young people.

Starmer has repeatedly argued that children deserve stronger protections online, including clearer age limits and tougher penalties for companies that fail to enforce them. Proposed measures could require platforms to verify users’ ages more rigorously and prevent minors from accessing content deemed inappropriate or harmful.

Supporters of the plan say such measures are long overdue, noting that many online services rely on self-reported age data that is easily bypassed. They argue that without stronger enforcement powers, regulators are left reacting to harm rather than preventing it.

Balancing safety and free expression

The proposed expansion of regulatory authority has sparked debate about its potential impact on free speech. Civil liberties groups caution that broader powers to regulate online access could lead to overreach, particularly if definitions of harmful content are vague or subject to political interpretation.

Critics worry that platforms, facing heavy fines or access restrictions, may err on the side of removing lawful but controversial content. This, they argue, could chill public debate and disproportionately affect marginalized voices.

Government officials have sought to reassure critics, saying safeguards will be built into any new framework to protect legitimate expression. Decisions would be subject to oversight, and regulators would be required to act proportionately, they say. Still, the balance between safety and freedom remains one of the most contentious aspects of the proposal.

Impact on technology companies

If enacted, the expanded powers would have significant implications for global technology firms operating in the UK. Companies could face higher compliance costs as they adapt systems, moderation practices, and reporting mechanisms to meet stricter standards.

Some firms have already invested heavily in content moderation and safety tools, but others warn that constant regulatory changes create uncertainty. Smaller platforms and startups, in particular, may struggle to absorb the costs associated with enhanced compliance.

Industry groups have called for clear guidance and consultation, arguing that effective regulation requires collaboration rather than confrontation. They also stress the need for international coordination, warning that fragmented national rules could complicate global operations and innovation.

The role of artificial intelligence

The rise of AI has added urgency to the debate over online regulation. Generative AI tools can produce realistic text, images, and videos at scale, raising concerns about misinformation, deepfakes, and automated harassment.

Starmer’s government has indicated that any expansion of regulatory powers would explicitly address AI-driven risks. This could include requirements for transparency around AI-generated content and obligations for companies to assess and mitigate systemic risks posed by their technologies.

Experts say the challenge lies in crafting rules flexible enough to adapt to rapidly evolving AI capabilities. Overly rigid regulations risk becoming obsolete, while vague rules may be difficult to enforce.

Political context and public pressure

Starmer’s push comes amid rising public concern about the influence of digital platforms on daily life. High-profile cases involving online abuse, extremist content, and data misuse have fueled demands for stronger government action.

Politically, the issue cuts across party lines, though approaches differ. While there is broad agreement on the need to address online harms, disagreements remain over how far regulation should go and how powers should be exercised.

For Starmer, demonstrating leadership on digital safety aligns with broader efforts to present his government as proactive and reform-oriented. Supporters say the initiative reflects a willingness to confront powerful tech interests, while critics view it as an expansion of state control.

International comparisons

The UK is not alone in seeking greater control over online platforms. Governments around the world are grappling with similar challenges, from misinformation to child safety and data protection. European regulators have moved toward stricter oversight, while other countries have adopted more hands-off approaches. The UK’s proposals are being closely watched as a potential model for balancing innovation with accountability. Analysts note that the effectiveness of any regulatory regime will depend not only on the scope of powers granted, but also on the resources and expertise available to enforce them.

Next steps and legislative path

Details of Starmer’s proposed expansion of regulatory powers are expected to be developed through consultations with industry, civil society, and legal experts. Any changes would likely require parliamentary approval, setting the stage for intense debate. Lawmakers will scrutinize how new powers are defined, the thresholds for intervention, and the mechanisms for appeal and oversight. The government has indicated it is open to refining proposals in response to feedback, but remains committed to strengthening its hand. Timing will be critical. Officials say action is needed quickly to address emerging risks, but rushing legislation could increase the likelihood of legal challenges or unintended consequences.

A defining test for digital governance

Starmer’s effort to secure greater powers to regulate online access represents a defining moment in the UK’s approach to digital governance. It reflects a growing consensus that the internet’s benefits must be balanced with stronger protections against harm.

Whether the initiative succeeds will depend on its ability to command public trust, withstand legal scrutiny, and adapt to technological change. As debates continue, one thing is clear: the question of how much control governments should have over online spaces is set to remain at the center of political and social discourse.
