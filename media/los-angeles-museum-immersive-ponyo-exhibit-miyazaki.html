Los Angeles Museum Unveils Immersive 'Ponyo' Exhibit by Hayao Miyazaki

A Los Angeles museum exhibition explores Hayao Miyazaki's Ponyo through production artwork, interactive installations and large-scale animated environments. This guide explains what visitors can see and how the displays relate to the film.

A museum in Los Angeles has unveiled an immersive exhibition dedicated to Ponyo, inviting visitors into the vividly imagined world of legendary Japanese filmmaker Hayao Miyazaki. The exhibit transforms gallery spaces into flowing seascapes, hand-drawn environments, and interactive installations that reflect the warmth, wonder, and environmental themes at the heart of the beloved animated film.

The exhibition marks one of the most ambitious U.S. museum presentations devoted to Miyazaki’s work, underscoring the enduring appeal of his storytelling across generations and cultures. Designed to be experiential rather than archival, the show allows visitors to step inside the emotional and visual language of Ponyo, a film that has become a touchstone for family audiences since its release.

Bringing animation into physical space

Rather than focusing solely on finished film clips or static artwork, the exhibit reimagines Ponyo as a walk-through environment. Rooms are filled with softly lit ocean motifs, oversized character renderings, and soundscapes inspired by waves, wind, and childhood imagination.

Curators said the goal was to translate Miyazaki’s hand-drawn animation into a physical experience that captures the sensation of movement and wonder found in the film. Visitors can explore installations inspired by Ponyo’s underwater home, coastal towns, and the fantastical merging of land and sea that defines the story’s emotional arc.

The immersive approach reflects a growing trend among museums seeking to attract broader audiences by blending art, storytelling, and interactive design.

A celebration of Miyazaki’s themes

At its core, Ponyo tells a simple but profound story about friendship, family, and humanity’s relationship with nature. The exhibition emphasizes these themes, highlighting Miyazaki’s long-standing concern with environmental balance and childhood innocence.

Wall texts and multimedia displays explain how Ponyo fits within Miyazaki’s broader body of work, which often centers on young protagonists navigating complex worlds shaped by adult decisions. The exhibit avoids heavy academic framing, instead encouraging visitors to engage emotionally and intuitively with the material.

Museum educators say this approach mirrors Miyazaki’s own philosophy: that stories should be felt before they are analyzed.

Appeal across generations

Families with children make up a large portion of the exhibition’s audience, but organizers say the show was designed with adults in mind as well. Many visitors grew up watching Miyazaki’s films and now share them with their own children, creating a multi-generational connection that few animated works achieve.

By focusing on Ponyo—one of Miyazaki’s most accessible and visually playful films—the museum aimed to create an entry point for newcomers while offering longtime fans a fresh perspective. Early visitor response has highlighted the exhibition’s calming atmosphere, with many describing it as a welcome contrast to fast-paced digital entertainment.

Animation as fine art

The exhibition also reflects a broader shift in how animation is treated within cultural institutions. Once considered primarily commercial entertainment, animated films by Miyazaki and his collaborators at Studio Ghibli are increasingly recognized as works of fine art. Curators noted that Miyazaki’s meticulous hand-drawn style, emphasis on natural detail, and narrative restraint align closely with traditional artistic values. By presenting Ponyo in a museum setting, the exhibition places animation alongside painting, sculpture, and installation art. This recognition has grown steadily in recent years, as museums worldwide expand their definitions of visual culture.

Los Angeles as a fitting host

Los Angeles, long associated with film and animation, provides a natural setting for an exhibition celebrating cinematic art. The city’s diverse audiences and deep ties to the entertainment industry make it an ideal location for a show that bridges popular culture and museum practice.

Museum officials said hosting a Miyazaki-focused exhibition also reflects the city’s increasingly global cultural outlook, embracing storytelling traditions from beyond Hollywood while acknowledging animation’s influence on modern filmmaking.

Enduring magic

Decades into his career, Hayao Miyazaki remains one of the most influential figures in animation, and Ponyo continues to resonate for its gentle storytelling and emotional honesty. The Los Angeles exhibition offers a rare opportunity to experience that magic outside the cinema, transforming a beloved film into a shared physical journey. As visitors move through its dreamlike spaces, the exhibit reinforces what fans have long believed: Miyazaki’s worlds are not just meant to be watched, but felt.
