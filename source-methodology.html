Research Methodology

How we find, evaluate and present information in our articles.

CorporateWire is a blog, not a wire service or continuously updated publication. Our articles synthesize publicly available records, research and attributed third-party material. This page describes the standards we use to make that work useful and transparent.

Our Research Standards

Primary Records Where Available

We prefer company filings, court documents, legislation, regulator publications, official datasets, academic research and other first-party records. Links may point to a live collection when a stable document URL is unavailable.

Attribution for Third-Party Claims

When an article relies on another publication, analyst, organization or quoted speaker, it should name the source and make clear what that source actually supports.

Corroboration and Limitations

High-stakes or disputed claims should be corroborated when possible. If only one authoritative source is available, the article should identify it, explain the limits of what is known and avoid overstating certainty.

Conflicts and Commercial Material

Sources may have political, commercial or personal interests. We disclose material relationships when they affect how readers should evaluate a claim. Paid posts and affiliate relationships are labeled under our advertising policy.

Dates, Data and Changing Records

Figures should identify their reporting period and originating institution. Markets, filings and policy pages can change, so readers should confirm time-sensitive information against the dated record before acting on it.

Each post includes a research-resources module with relevant official collections. These links are a starting point for further reading, not a claim that every linked institution endorses the article.

For more details, see our Editorial Policy and Correction Policy.
