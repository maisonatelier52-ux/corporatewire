SpaceX Targets Historic IPO With Nearly $1.8 Trillion Valuation

SpaceX is reportedly preparing for a historic IPO that could value the company at up to $1.77 trillion, making it one of the largest public offerings ever.

Christopher Reed | Lead Business & Economy Investigative Reporter | June. 5, 2026
SHARE

Elon Musk’s SpaceX is preparing for what could become the largest initial public offering ever attempted.

The company is reportedly targeting a valuation between $1.75 trillion and $1.77 trillion ahead of its long-awaited market debut. If achieved, the figure would instantly place SpaceX among the most valuable publicly traded companies on Earth. The numbers are staggering.

Reports indicate SpaceX plans to offer roughly 555.6 million shares at a fixed price of $135 each, raising about $75 billion in fresh capital. That would shatter previous IPO fundraising records and mark a defining moment for both Wall Street and the commercial space industry.

Investors appear willing to embrace the scale.

SpaceX has evolved far beyond a rocket company. It now sits at the intersection of aerospace, telecommunications, defense technology and emerging artificial intelligence infrastructure. Few companies offer exposure to all four sectors through a single investment.

The proposed valuation far exceeds previous private-market estimates. Proponents claim this is evidence of the company’s growing influence across a range of industries. Detractors see a different story. They question whether even a behemoth like SpaceX deserves a valuation close to $2 trillion.

The IPO is expected to begin trading on Nasdaq in mid-June.

One unusual detail has drawn attention. SpaceX reportedly plans to use a fixed share price instead of the traditional pricing range used in most public offerings. The approach suggests strong confidence in demand. The company is also expected to reserve a meaningful portion of shares for retail investors, giving individual traders access to one of the most anticipated listings in years.

For Wall Street, this is more than another IPO. It is a test of how much investors are willing to pay for the future.

SpaceX Listing Could Push Musk Toward Trillionaire Status

The offering is not just about SpaceX.

It is also about Elon Musk.

The proposed valuation has reignited discussion about whether Musk could become the world's first trillionaire. Much of his wealth remains tied to his ownership stake in SpaceX, meaning any successful public listing would dramatically increase the value of his holdings.

According to reports, a valuation near the upper end of the proposed range could push Musk's personal fortune toward or beyond the $1 trillion mark.

That would represent a financial milestone with little historical precedent.

Over the past decade, Musk has transformed himself from a successful entrepreneur into one of the wealthiest individuals in modern history through major stakes in Tesla, SpaceX and several other ventures. The SpaceX IPO could become the single largest contributor to that wealth accumulation.

The prospect has sparked familiar debates.

Supporters say Musk’s fortune is the product of decades of innovation, risk-taking and business execution. Critics point to growing concerns about wealth concentration and the influence that extraordinary fortunes can exert over markets, industries and public policy.

Those arguments are unlikely to disappear.

As investors evaluate SpaceX, they are also watching the creation of what could become an entirely new category of personal wealth.

Starlink Emerges as the Company's Financial Powerhouse

Rocket launches built SpaceX's reputation. Starlink is increasingly driving its valuation.

The satellite internet business has quickly become one of the most important growth engines for the company, providing recurring subscription revenue while quickly expanding into international markets.

Investors are paying attention.

Launch operations are contract-by-contract, mission-by-mission, but Starlink provides a steadier stream of revenue. That recurring revenue model has become a major factor supporting bullish forecasts for future earnings.

SpaceX's broader business remains strong as well.

The company remains a leader in commercial launch services and has won government and defense contracts that provide more stability of revenue. Launch operations, satellite deployments and national security projects have all increased investor confidence. Recent disclosures showed revenue growing about 33% in 2025 to nearly $19 billion.

Profitability is still an afterthought for many investors. The company is still spending heavily on future projects and expansion initiatives. Rather than worry about current losses, many shareholders seem more interested in long-term growth prospects.

Artificial intelligence has added another layer to the story.

Investors increasingly see value in the combination of satellite communications, data infrastructure and AI-related technologies. Few competitors operate across all three areas simultaneously. That broader technology footprint has helped transform SpaceX from an aerospace company into one of the most closely watched growth businesses in the world.

Massive Valuation Sparks Debate Across Wall Street

Excitement is abundant. Questions are growing.

A valuation approaching $1.75 trillion requires extraordinary assumptions about future growth, revenue expansion and profitability. Not every analyst is convinced those expectations are realistic.

Some say SpaceX deserves a premium because traditional valuation models fail to capture the unique positioning of the company in aerospace, telecommunications and defense and technology markets.

Others remain cautious.

The proposed valuation suggests revenue multiples significantly higher than those attributed to many established public companies, several analysts have said. Morningstar, among those reportedly suggesting a substantially lower fair value estimate, based its view on uncertainty surrounding future projects and long-term earnings projections.

The debate reflects a familiar pattern.

Markets often reward dominant companies with aggressive valuations when investors believe future opportunities are large enough. The challenge comes later, when those expectations must be met.

The scale of the offering raises the stakes.

Big tech IPOs tend to swing in the days after listing as investors recalibrate their views of the growth story and financial metrics. Given the size of SpaceX, a big move in the stock could impact broader tech and growth sentiment.

Despite those concerns, demand appears exceptionally strong.

The company enters the public market with enormous momentum, a powerful brand and a business portfolio unlike almost any other public corporation.

Whether the valuation proves justified remains an open question.

The significance of the IPO does not.

SpaceX is preparing to open a new chapter in its history. Wall Street is preparing to place a price on the future of commercial space, global connectivity and one of the most ambitious companies ever built.
