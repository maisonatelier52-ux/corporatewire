Federal Judge Rules Trump $10B IRS Lawsuit Was Filed for 'Improper Purpose,' Refers Lawyer Todd Blanche for Discipline

A federal judge delivered a scathing rebuke of Donald Trump's $10 billion lawsuit against the IRS and Department of Justice over leaked tax records, dismissing the case with prejudice as an 'improper and harassing' filing and referring lead counsel Todd Blanche to state bar ethics committees for potential discipline.

Nicole Patterson | Staff Writer | July. 14, 2026
SHARE

MIAMI & WASHINGTON — In an extraordinarily sharp judicial rebuke carrying profound ramifications for executive legal ethics, Senior U.S. District Judge K. Michael Moore delivered a blistering 48-page ruling on Friday, holding that Donald Trump's $10 billion lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service and Department of Justice was filed for an "improper and harassing purpose." Sparing no words in his reprimand, the federal judge dismissed the lawsuit with prejudice and formally referred Trump's lead trial attorney, former federal prosecutor and incoming Deputy Attorney General nominee Todd Blanche, to the Southern District of Florida's Ad Hoc Committee on Attorney Admissions and Grievances for potential formal disciplinary sanctions.

What Happened: Federal Judge Rebukes $10 Billion Tax Leak Lawsuit

The high-profile lawsuit arose from the criminal disclosures orchestrated by former Booz Allen Hamilton contractor Charles Littlejohn, who leaked decades of Trump's confidential personal and corporate tax filings to media organizations.

Rather than pursuing conventional statutory damages under Internal Revenue Code Section 7431 — which caps statutory recovery at $1,000 per unauthorized disclosure or actual demonstrable damages — Trump's legal counsel filed an audacious $10 billion claim asserting broad tort violations and governmental conspiracy. Judge Moore concluded that the exorbitant figure was not grounded in financial reality, but designed as an instrument of political theater and intimidation against career civil servants.

"The record unequivocally demonstrates that this action was commenced not to remedy cognizable legal harm, but to utilize the federal judiciary as an arena for public grievance and political spectacle," Judge Moore wrote in his scathing opinion. "Such frivolous pleadings erode public reverence for the rule of law and cannot be indulged by this Court."

Ethical Fallout: Todd Blanche Referred for Professional Discipline

The judicial referral hits Todd Blanche at a uniquely sensitive political juncture, creating significant complications for his Senate confirmation proceedings:

  • Violation of Rule 11: The court found that Blanche and his co-counsel violated Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11(b), which mandates that attorneys certify that all factual contentions possess evidentiary support and are not advanced for improper harassment.
  • Bar Grievance Inquiries: The district court's formal referral requires the disciplinary committee to determine whether Blanche should face public reprimand, monetary sanctions, or suspension from federal bar practice.
  • Senate Judiciary Scrutiny: Lawmakers on the Senate Judiciary Committee immediately announced plans to question Blanche under oath regarding Judge Moore's ethical findings during confirmation hearings for Department of Justice leadership roles.

Why It Matters: Establishing Limits on Executive Litigation Tactics

Constitutional law scholars and civil litigation specialists emphasize that Judge Moore's decision sets a crucial judicial boundary against weaponizing multi-billion-dollar federal tort claims:

By strictly applying Rule 11 sanctions, the court signaled that high-profile political figures cannot insulate their legal counsel from professional accountability when advancing claims unsupported by recognized statutory frameworks. Legal defense teams for executive branch agencies noted that the dismissal shields the Treasury and IRS from speculative mass-damage liabilities.

What to Watch Next: Appeals to the Eleventh Circuit and Confirmation Politics

Attorneys representing Trump announced their intention to immediately appeal Judge Moore's dismissal and sanctions referral to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit in Atlanta, arguing that the court demonstrated prejudice and denied the plaintiff fundamental due process rights.

As the appeal advances, legal observers will watch whether Blanche's legal defense team seeks an expedited stay of bar disciplinary proceedings pending appellate review.
