Bargain shoppers win, while software stocks wobble

The latest round of company earnings shows an economy where shoppers are hunting for deals and investors are getting pickier about technology stocks. Discount retailer Ross Stores reported strong traffic and beat its own forecast. Intuit, the maker of TurboTax and QuickBooks, lowered its yearly revenue outlook and agreed to sell Mailchimp, and its shares dropped. Meanwhile, Keysight, a maker of electronic test equipment, posted a big jump in sales. Here is a plain-English look at what these results say about the business world right now.

Ross Stores Beats Forecasts as Shoppers Keep Coming

On November 19, Ross Stores reported third-quarter sales of $6.3 billion, up 12% from a year earlier. Sales at stores open at least a year rose 8%, driven mainly by more customers walking through the door, on top of a 7% gain last year. Earnings were $1.91 a share, compared with $1.58 a year ago and above the company's own forecast of $1.75 to $1.83. The off-price chain, which sells brand-name clothing and home goods at a discount, opened 51 stores in the quarter and now has 2,377. For the holiday quarter, Ross expects comparable sales to grow 4% to 5%, and it raised its full-year earnings forecast to $8.75 to $8.84 a share. That outlook includes about 60 cents a share from tariff refunds booked earlier in the year. CEO Jim Conroy said, We are extremely pleased with our third quarter sales and earnings growth. It is another sign that value-focused shoppers are still spending, just more carefully.

Intuit Lowers Its Outlook and Sells Mailchimp

Also on November 19, Intuit reported first-quarter revenue of about $4.35 billion, up 12%, with earnings per share of $2.91 on an adjusted basis. The results were above the top of its own guidance, but investors focused on the outlook. Intuit lowered its full-year revenue forecast to between $23.08 billion and $23.37 billion, reflecting changes at Credit Karma, and its shares fell roughly 9% to 11% in the following session, according to market reports. The company also agreed to sell Mailchimp, its email marketing business, to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion, made up of $3.31 billion in cash and $368 million in Bending Spoons stock. The deal is expected to close in early 2027, and Intuit plans to use the proceeds for share buybacks. CEO Sasan Goodarzi said, We delivered a solid first quarter, with results above the top end of our guidance.

Keysight Reports Revenue Up 41%

Keysight Technologies, which makes the equipment used to test electronics and communications networks, reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $1.99 billion, up 41% from a year earlier and ahead of the roughly $1.95 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings were $3.61 a share, compared with expectations of $3.37. Orders for the full year grew 49%, a sign of strong demand from customers building communications and computing infrastructure. For the current quarter, the company forecast revenue of $2.04 billion to $2.06 billion. Strong results like this show that spending on the equipment behind the AI and data boom is still holding up.

What to Watch Next

The coming days bring Black Friday on November 27, which will show whether the shoppers behind Ross's strong quarter keep spending as prices stay high. Investors will also watch whether other software companies follow Intuit in lowering forecasts, and whether the Mailchimp sale closes on schedule in early 2027.
