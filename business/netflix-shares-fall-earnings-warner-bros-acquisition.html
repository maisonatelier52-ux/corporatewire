Netflix Shares Dip Despite Earnings Beat as $72B Warner Bros. Discovery Bid Pauses Buybacks

Netflix shares slid 6% despite beating fourth-quarter earnings estimates as the streaming giant unveiled an audacious $72 billion cash-and-stock takeover offer for Warner Bros. Discovery, immediately pausing share repurchases to fund the entertainment industry's largest megamerger.

Christopher Reed | Lead Business & Economy Investigative Reporter | Feb. 24, 2026
SHARE

LOS GATOS & NEW YORK — Netflix Inc. (NASDAQ: NFLX) saw its shares decline 6.2% on Wednesday despite reporting fourth-quarter financial results that beat Wall Street consensus on revenue and subscriber additions, as investor enthusiasm was overshadowed by the company's unexpected announcement that it will pause its multi-billion-dollar share repurchase program to finance an ambitious $72 billion cash-and-stock takeover bid for media giant Warner Bros. Discovery Inc. (NASDAQ: WBD). The blockbuster merger proposal would unite Hollywood's premier streaming platform with iconic franchises including HBO, Warner Bros. Studios, and DC Studios.

What Happened: Strong Earnings Eclipsed by a $72 Billion Megamerger

On an operational basis, Netflix delivered an exceptional fourth quarter, generating $10.8 billion in quarterly revenue and adding 13.1 million net paid subscribers worldwide, driven by the global smash success of non-English content and rapid expansion of its ad-supported tier.

However, Wall Street equity analysts immediately turned their attention to the capital allocation shock triggered by co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters. Netflix confirmed it has submitted a formal merger proposal valuing Warner Bros. Discovery at $28.50 per share — a 42% premium over its undisturbed trading price. To fund the cash component of the transaction, Netflix announced the immediate suspension of its share buyback program and plans to arrange up to $35 billion in syndicated investment-grade bank debt.

"While bringing HBO's prestige intellectual property and Warner Bros.' century-old film vault under Netflix's distribution network is strategically alluring, equity investors are rightfully skittish about taking on tens of billions in leverage in an elevated interest rate environment," explained Laura Henderson, senior media analyst at Morgan Stanley.

Strategic Imperatives: HBO Prestige, DC Studios, and Global Streaming Scale

If completed, the combination would create an entertainment colossus commanding over 410 million global direct-to-consumer subscribers, vastly outpacing rivals Walt Disney Co. and Amazon Prime Video.

Netflix leadership argued that acquiring WBD solves critical long-term structural needs. Netflix would secure perpetual ownership over foundational franchises including Harry Potter, Game of Thrones, the DC Comics cinematic universe, and extensive live sports broadcast rights via TNT Sports. Furthermore, uniting the technical infrastructure of Max with Netflix's industry-leading recommendation algorithms would generate an estimated $3.5 billion in annual cost and operational synergies within twenty-four months of closing.

Regulatory Obstacles: Antitrust Scrutiny Looms in Washington and Brussels

The primary hurdle confronting the megadeal lies not in corporate boardrooms, but in federal antitrust enforcement offices:

  • Department of Justice Antitrust Review: U.S. antitrust regulators are anticipated to review whether combining the world's largest streaming service with one of Hollywood's major theatrical and television production studios harms independent content creators.
  • European Commission Scrutiny: Brussels regulators are expected to demand structural concessions or divestitures, particularly regarding European production quotas and regional broadcasting rights.
  • Linear Cable Television Spinoffs: Industry insiders expect Netflix to carve out and spin off WBD's legacy linear cable assets (including CNN, TBS, and HGTV) into an independent entity, ensuring Netflix remains a pure-play digital platform.

Market Outlook: Debt Ratings, Buyback Pauses, and Shareholder Next Steps

Credit rating agencies including Moody's and S&P Global placed Netflix's senior unsecured debt ratings on review for downgrade, cautioning that pro-forma net leverage could climb to 3.8x EBITDA.

For Warner Bros. Discovery shareholders, the takeover offer represents an attractive monetization exit following years of post-spinoff share price erosion. Both boards are scheduled to convene special shareholder meetings in mid-August, with the regulatory review process expected to extend through mid-2027.

The Paramount offer includes the merged company's television and news holdings, whereas Netflix is solely bidding on Warner Bros.' film and streaming assets. When asked by Guggenheim analyst Mike Morris on the call if Netflix planned to raise rates, co-CEO Gregory Peters answered, "There is no impact or change to our approach and how we're running the business in that regard."

It also intends to continue the usual launch of Warner Brothers pictures, with a 45-day window in theaters before moving to streaming. When asked about regulatory permission, Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos stated, "We've already made progress towards securing the necessary regulatory approvals." He continued, "Our deal strengthens the marketplace, and it ensures healthy competition that will benefit consumers and protect and create jobs. That's why we're confident in the approval."
