Walmart, Target and TJX Raise Outlooks as Shoppers Turn More Selective for the Holidays

Walmart raises its outlook for the third time, Target traffic climbs 5% and TJX jumps, while Lowe's margins feel the squeeze from fuel costs and Home Depot slips.

Christopher Reed | Business & Economy Contributor | Published November 20, 2026

Retailers give a clear read on the American shopper

This week brought the biggest test yet of how American households are coping with high prices. Walmart, Target, TJX, Lowe's and Home Depot all reported quarterly results within days of each other, and together they paint a fairly consistent picture. Shoppers are still spending, but they are being choosy, hunting for bargains and gravitating towards stores that offer low prices and convenience. Most of the big chains lifted their forecasts, though higher fuel and transport costs are squeezing profit margins for some. With the holiday shopping season only days away, here's a plain-English look at what the biggest US retailers said, what it means for your wallet and what to watch next.

Walmart Raises Its Outlook for the Third Time This Year

On Thursday, Walmart reported third-quarter sales of $188.8 billion, up 5.2% and ahead of the $187.38 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings were 65 cents a share against 64 cents forecast, although earnings under standard accounting rules fell to 62 cents from 77 cents a year earlier. Global e-commerce sales rose 17%, and US e-commerce was up 23%, with more than 80% of US online orders fulfilled from stores. Comparable sales rose 2.9%, up from 2.6% last quarter, which had been Walmart's weakest growth in six years. The company lifted its full-year earnings forecast to a range of $2.84 to $2.89 a share and its sales growth forecast to 4.9% to 5.2%, although both were slightly more cautious than Wall Street had hoped. CEO John Furner said, We delivered strong results, growing our core business and gaining market share. Shares rose nearly 1% before the opening bell.

Why Higher-Income Shoppers Are Choosing Walmart

One of the more interesting details in Walmart's report is who is shopping there. The retailer said its biggest market share gains have come from households earning more than $100,000 a year, which suggests that even people with comfortable incomes are looking for ways to save. Consumers have stayed resilient despite painful inflation, but they are more selective about what they buy, and many treat Walmart as a one-stop shop for groceries, low-priced goods and pharmaceuticals. Official figures back this up. Retail sales rose just 0.2% in October, down from 0.8% in September, as Americans pulled back on cars, clothing, furniture and hobbies. Walmart is also trying to keep customers loyal, with a new annual bundle of Walmart+ and Sam's Club Plus for $120, which it says saves nearly $100 compared with buying both separately.

Target's Store Traffic Jumps 5%

Target had one of the more encouraging reports of the week. On Wednesday, it said third-quarter sales rose 7.1% to $27.1 billion, with comparable sales up 5.6%. More footfall did most of the work, as store traffic climbed 5% and digital comparable sales rose 9.8%. The company raised its full-year sales outlook, and its shares gained about 4.5% on the day. It is a strong set of numbers for a retailer that has been under pressure to win shoppers back. For the holidays, Target is leaning on affordability, exclusive products and promotions, and it is adding more than 8,000 new gifts, including thousands priced at $5 or less. Wayfair, the online home goods seller, also reported earlier this month that US revenue rose 11.4% and that it plans to spend more on advertising in the fourth quarter.

TJX Jumps While Lowe's and Home Depot Feel the Squeeze

Not every retailer had an easy week. TJX, the parent of TJ Maxx and Marshalls, lifted its full-year outlook and said sales momentum should improve into the holidays, sending its shares up almost 9%. Lowe's beat on adjusted earnings, but its gross margin fell from a year earlier because higher fuel and transportation costs and acquisition-related costs outweighed the benefit of tariff refunds, and its shares edged up 0.8%. Home Depot reported third-quarter sales of $43.4 billion, up 4.9%, and said it now expects about 4.5% sales growth this year, the top of its earlier range, but its stock still closed down about 2.5%. It has also said its forecast includes tariff refunds, which it expects to partly offset unexpected fuel, energy and other input costs. Richard McPhail takes over as Home Depot's CEO on November 23.

What the Holiday Forecasts Say

The retail industry is cautiously optimistic about the season ahead. The National Retail Federation expects November and December sales to grow 4.2% from last year to about $1.013 trillion, compared with a 3.1% rise to $972 billion in 2025. Adobe forecasts a record $275.1 billion in US online spending between November 1 and December 31, up 6.7% from a year earlier. Those figures are estimates, not results, and inflation means part of any increase in dollars simply reflects higher prices. Black Friday falls on November 27, and Macy's, which holds its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade the day before, reports earnings on December 2. If you're shopping, comparing prices across stores, checking for tariff-related price changes and using store loyalty offers can help your money go further.

What to Watch Next for US Retailers

There are several things to keep an eye on. First, how Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales compare with the forecasts, since early discounting could eat into profit margins. Second, fuel and transportation costs, which are already weighing on Lowe's and Home Depot and could hit other retailers in the fourth quarter. Third, the Federal Reserve's December 8 and 9 meeting, because another interest rate hike would raise borrowing costs for shoppers and retailers alike. Finally, listen out for more tariff refund news, which is already helping some companies' earnings. For households, the key takeaway is that stores are competing hard for your money, so it pays to shop around and set a budget before the holiday rush.
