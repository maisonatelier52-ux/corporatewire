Chinese AI Models Festoon Spring Festival a Year After DeepSeek Shock

Chinese AI models have taken center stage during the Spring Festival, exactly a year after DeepSeek first shocked the market.

Amanda Whitaker | Senior Financial & Healthcare Correspondent • Feb. 20, 2026
SHARE

From chatbots to digital hosts, artificial intelligence takes center stage in China’s biggest holiday

Artificial intelligence was everywhere during China’s Spring Festival celebrations this year, marking a striking contrast from just a year ago when the emergence of DeepSeek sent shockwaves through the global AI community. From AI-powered virtual performers to smart travel planning tools, Chinese-made AI models played a prominent role in the country’s most important annual holiday.

The widespread presence of domestic AI technology highlights how quickly China’s AI ecosystem has evolved since DeepSeek’s debut, which challenged assumptions about China’s ability to compete with leading Western models. Over the past year, Chinese tech firms have accelerated development, deployment, and commercialization of large language models, bringing them directly into everyday consumer experiences.

AI becomes part of holiday life

During the Spring Festival, AI-powered applications were integrated into a range of activities, including online shopping promotions, travel planning, entertainment, and digital red envelope exchanges. Major platforms used AI-generated content to personalize holiday greetings, recommend gifts, and provide real-time customer service.

State media outlets also experimented with AI-generated presenters and interactive features, blending tradition with cutting-edge technology. For many users, AI tools were no longer novel experiments but familiar companions embedded in widely used apps.

Industry analysts say the festival served as a real-world stress test for Chinese AI models, demonstrating their ability to handle massive user traffic while delivering localized and culturally tailored content.

One year after the DeepSeek moment

DeepSeek’s release last year was widely viewed as a turning point, challenging perceptions that China lagged significantly behind U.S. leaders in advanced AI. Its performance and cost efficiency sparked renewed competition across China’s technology sector and prompted increased investment in domestic AI research.

Since then, major Chinese tech firms have rolled out their own large language models, focusing on practical applications rather than purely experimental capabilities. The emphasis has been on scalability, regulatory compliance, and integration with existing digital ecosystems. A year on, the Spring Festival showcased how far these efforts have progressed, with AI moving from labs into mainstream consumer use.

Regulation shapes deployment

China’s regulatory framework has played a significant role in how AI models are deployed. Developers are required to adhere to content controls, data security standards, and transparency rules, shaping the tone and functionality of consumer-facing AI tools.

While these rules limit certain uses, they have also encouraged companies to focus on enterprise services, productivity tools, and culturally aligned consumer applications. During the festival, AI content was tightly moderated, reflecting the balance authorities seek between innovation and control.

Experts say this regulatory clarity has helped accelerate adoption by reducing uncertainty for developers and users alike.

Economic and cultural implications

The prominence of AI during the Spring Festival underscores its growing economic importance. Tech companies used the holiday to showcase new features, attract users, and strengthen brand loyalty. Retailers and advertisers leveraged AI to boost engagement during one of the busiest shopping periods of the year.

Culturally, the integration of AI into a tradition-centered holiday reflects how deeply digital technology has become woven into daily life in China. What once might have been seen as a novelty is now part of how millions celebrate, communicate, and consume.

Global competition intensifies

China’s rapid progress in deploying homegrown AI models comes as global competition in artificial intelligence intensifies. While U.S. firms continue to lead in foundational research, China’s focus on large-scale deployment and real-world applications is drawing attention from international observers. Analysts say the Spring Festival provided a visible reminder that AI leadership is not just about technical benchmarks, but also about adoption at scale. A year after the DeepSeek shock, China’s AI models are no longer surprising outsiders—they are becoming an expected part of the country’s digital landscape, signaling a new phase in the global AI race.
