Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Resigns as Data Center Backlash and a Microsoft Outage Rattle Big Tech

Jensen Huang steps down as Nvidia CEO, Microsoft 365 suffers a major outage, new governors threaten to pause data centers, and Tesla winds down the Cybertruck.

Matthew Collins | Technology & Corporate Affairs Contributor • November 13, 2026

What to know

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO on November 12, and CFO Colette Kress is interim chief. The news hit a tech sector already dealing with a Microsoft 365 outage, a growing state-level push to pause new data centers and Tesla's plan to end Cybertruck production.

A turbulent week for American tech

It has been one of those weeks when the tech industry doesn't get a moment to breathe. The company that powers the AI boom just lost its famous CEO. Millions of office workers were locked out of their email and chat. Newly elected governors are talking about slowing down the data centers that AI depends on. And Tesla is quietly closing the book on one of its most talked-about products. None of these stories is small, and together they say a lot about how fragile the AI boom feels right now. Here's a plain-English look at the biggest technology stories in the US and what they could mean for investors, workers and everyday users.

Jensen Huang Steps Down as Nvidia CEO

The biggest story came on Thursday, November 12, when Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. The announcement followed days of reports about his personal life. The New York Times reported a second extramarital affair, and Reuters reported a third later in the day, with one reported partner said to be someone US intelligence suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence. Nvidia's board named chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim CEO while it searches for a permanent successor. Investors reacted fast. Shares fell more than 10% before the open and by as much as 13% early in the session, before closing down 4.34% at $166.19. The stock is now down roughly 30% over the past month. For a company at the center of the AI boom, losing the founder-led face of its brand is a big deal, and the next test comes soon, with Nvidia due to report quarterly earnings on November 17.

Wall Street Slips as Treasury Yields Cross 5%

The Nvidia news weighed on the whole market. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.39% to 25,366.09, the S&P 500 lost 0.35% to 7,592.40 and the Dow dropped 0.36% to 52,297.09, while smaller companies took a harder hit, with the Russell 2000 down 1.66%. The 10-year Treasury yield also rose above 5% as traders weighed the chance of more Fed rate hikes. There was one bright spot in the AI trade. Chipmaker Cerebras jumped about 15% after hours after reporting record third-quarter revenue, raising its full-year outlook and announcing deals with two new hyperscalers. It's a useful reminder that the AI story isn't over, even if the leadership at its biggest name is in flux.

Microsoft 365 Outage Knocks Out Outlook, Teams and Copilot

If your email and chat stopped working on Thursday, you weren't alone. A widespread Microsoft 365 outage disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot for tens of thousands of users across the US, and some reports said it dragged on for hours. Microsoft said it was investigating a problem affecting mailbox access in Exchange Online and was working on a fix. For many businesses, it was a sharp reminder of how much daily work now depends on a handful of cloud services. When a single provider has a bad day, meetings get cancelled, deadlines slip and customers wait. Outages like this also raise a bigger question for companies that are rushing to add AI assistants to every workflow. If the tools are this central, how prepared are they when something goes wrong?

Data Center Backlash Grows After the Midterms

Another storyline to watch is the growing political pushback against data centers. According to analysis by energy research firm Cleanview, nine states now have a governor or governor-elect who has promised or already ordered a pause on new data centers. Together they hold about 158.6 gigawatts, or 41% of the planned US pipeline. Texas, Georgia, Ohio and New Mexico are among the states named. Most of the proposals are pauses rather than outright bans, and they mainly affect projects that haven't started construction, so the real impact will depend on what state legislatures do next year. Local voters have also been voicing concern, with ballot measures going against data centers in at least 36 of 41 local elections, driven largely by worries about electricity prices and power demand. For tech giants that have promised hundreds of billions of dollars on AI infrastructure, that's a real headache.

Tesla Winds Down the Cybertruck

Elon Musk confirmed this week that Tesla will end Cybertruck production in about eight months. In a post on X, he said the company will try to build as many trucks as it can before then and will have to return deposits for a lot of people. He argued the truck isn't unprofitable, saying \"opportunity cost of the complexity of Cybertruck is vastly outweighed by the value of incremental Cybercab production.\" In other words, Tesla wants the factory space to build more of its two-seater Cybercab robotaxis. The numbers show how far reality fell from the early hype. Musk once predicted annual sales of around 250,000 trucks, while industry experts estimate about 71,000 have been sold since late 2023. Separately, Bloomberg reported that merger talks between SpaceX and Tesla are progressing, though an announcement could slip into 2027.

What to Watch Next in Tech

So what comes next? First, Nvidia's earnings on November 17 will show whether the company's business is holding up despite the leadership shake-up, and whether the board names a permanent CEO soon. Second, keep an eye on interest rates. With yields above 5% and traders expecting a December Fed hike, high-growth tech stocks could stay under pressure. Third, watch how state governments handle the data center debate once their legislatures meet. If the AI boom runs into limits on power and permits, the effects could spread well beyond Silicon Valley. As always, big swings are part of the picture right now, so avoid making big decisions based on a single day's headlines.
