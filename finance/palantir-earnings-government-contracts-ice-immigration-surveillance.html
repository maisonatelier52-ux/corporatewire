Palantir Reports $1.41B Revenue Amid Scrutiny of Defense and ICE Contracts

Palantir Technologies surged 8% after reporting quarterly results above analysts' expectations with record sales of $1.41 billion, driven by commercial AI demand and expanded homeland defense analytics contracts despite continuing civil-liberties scrutiny.

DENVER — Enterprise data analytics and artificial intelligence titan Palantir Technologies Inc. (NYSE: PLTR) delivered blowout fourth-quarter financial results that shattered Wall Street projections, sending its shares surging 8% in heavy after-hours trading. Driven by explosive commercial adoption of its proprietary Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) and expanded federal defense operations, quarterly revenue vaulted 70% year-over-year to a record $1.41 billion, surpassing the $1.33 billion consensus estimate, while adjusted earnings per share reached $0.25.

What Happened: The Anatomy of Palantir's Record-Breaking Quarter

Chief Executive Officer Alex Karp heralded the quarter as "one of the truly iconic performances in modern corporate history." In an emphatic letter to shareholders, Karp highlighted that U.S. commercial revenue surged over 64%, reflecting unprecedented enterprise eagerness to embed Palantir's AIP ontology directly into operational decision trees:

Operating income reached $412 million on an adjusted basis, delivering a robust 29% operating margin. Palantir closed 124 separate contracts valued at over $1 million each during the quarter, with 36 deals exceeding $10 million, demonstrating sticky enterprise demand across healthcare, automotive manufacturing, and defense logistics.

Government Dominance: Mission-Critical Contracts and Border Scrutiny

While commercial expansion drove percentage gains, Palantir's bedrock government business expanded by 48% to reach $785 million during the quarter:

  • Department of Defense AI Scaling: Palantir secured multi-year contract expansions with the U.S. Army and Special Operations Command to power algorithmic targeting and battlefield telemetry via its Maven Smart System.
  • Homeland Security & ICE Engagements: Civil liberties advocacy coalitions renewed protests regarding Palantir's multi-million-dollar analytic support contracts with Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Customs and Border Protection (CBP) under the Trump administration's expanded border enforcement mandate.
  • Karp's Unapologetic Defense: Karp directly defended the company's federal work on the earnings call, reiterating: "We did this while supporting some of the most intricate, vital national security operations the U.S. government has ever conducted."

Valuation Debate: Can High Multiples Withstand Tech Volatility?

Palantir's post-earnings rally pushed the software giant's enterprise value above $160 billion, igniting intense debate among Wall Street valuation analysts:

Trading at more than 32 times forward sales, Palantir commands one of the steepest valuation premiums in the software industry, far eclipsing peers such as Snowflake, Datadog, and Microsoft. Bullish analysts at Wedbush argue that Palantir is the undisputed "Messi of AI," capturing outsized enterprise budgets as companies transition from speculative AI pilots to mission-critical operational deployments.

What to Watch Next: S&P 500 Capital Inflows and 2026 Guidance

Looking ahead, Palantir raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to between $5.8 billion and $6.0 billion, signaling sustained 40%+ organic growth:

Institutional portfolio managers are watching whether Palantir's increasing weighting inside benchmark indices will trigger mandatory index fund inflows, while international defense ministries in the UK, Ukraine, and NATO allies are expected to finalize bilateral software licensing agreements before the end of the second quarter.
