Mortgage Rates Ease to 7.21% as Bitcoin ETFs Post Record Outflows

Mortgage rates dip to 7.21% before Thanksgiving, shoppers head into Black Friday with record buy-now-pay-later use expected, and US bitcoin ETFs suffer their biggest monthly outflow on record.

Your money this holiday weekend

US markets are closed for Thanksgiving and shoppers are heading into the busiest spending weekend of the year. Money matters are on many minds. Mortgage rates have eased a little but are still high, forecasters expect record online spending and buy-now-pay-later use on Black Friday, and bitcoin funds have seen a record run of money leaving. Here is a plain-English guide to the latest US finance news and what it could mean for your wallet.

Mortgage Rates Dip to 7.21%

Freddie Mac said on November 25 that the average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage fell to 7.21% from 7.28% the week before. A year ago it was 6.23%. The average 15-year rate fell to 6.54% from 6.66%, compared with 5.51% a year earlier. Freddie Mac chief economist Sam Khater said, Mortgage rates moved back down this week, offering modest relief for prospective homebuyers. The relief is small, and borrowers are still paying about a full percentage point more than a year ago. The survey covers borrowers who put 20% down and have excellent credit, so your own rate may be higher.

Black Friday Shoppers Spend With Care

Forecasts for Black Friday point to strong online sales even though shoppers are watching their budgets. Adobe expects Black Friday online spending of $12.9 billion, up 9.2%, and Cyber Monday spending of $15.1 billion, up 6.2%. It also expects buy-now-pay-later spending to reach a record $21.3 billion, with 82% of it on mobile, and discounts to be roughly flat on last year, with electronics at up to 30% off. Visa expects holiday retail sales to rise 4.5%, or only 1.1% after inflation, and PwC puts average gift spending at $708, down 2%. PwC's Ali Furman summed up the mood: The vibe says cutback. The cart says otherwise. Shoppers are also stocking up on everyday basics such as clothing, hygiene products and pet supplies during the sales.

Bitcoin ETFs Post Their Biggest Monthly Outflow on Record

US spot bitcoin ETFs have lost a net $5.39 billion in November, according to SoSoValue data through November 25 reported by CoinDesk. That is already the biggest monthly outflow since the funds launched in January 2024, beating the previous record of $4.51 billion set in June 2026. Bitcoin itself has fallen about 22% this month to around $60,000. Ether ETFs also lost $1.30 billion. CoinDesk says the outflows point to shrinking institutional demand, and it links them to expected Federal Reserve rate hikes, since higher rates make riskier assets less attractive. If the year ends with net outflows, 2026 would be the first calendar year of net outflows for the funds. The figures could change before month-end.

What to Watch Next

Keep an eye on three things. First, Friday's shortened trading session, with US stock markets closing at 1:00 p.m. Eastern, and how retailers and online sales hold up over the weekend and on Cyber Monday. Second, whether mortgage rates keep easing or turn back up as the Fed's December 8 to 9 meeting nears, with a quarter-point hike widely expected. Third, whether bitcoin ETF flows stabilise. If you are shopping, compare prices and be careful with buy-now-pay-later plans. If you are buying a home, get a few lender quotes. Check the latest figures before you publish.
