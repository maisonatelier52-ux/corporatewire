Global Stocks Slide and Oil Prices Jump After Trump Warns Iran That 'The Clock Is Ticking'

Asian and European equity indices retreated sharply while Brent crude spiked over 4% after President Donald Trump issued a stern ultimatum to Tehran, elevating concerns of military escalation and severe disruptions to Strait of Hormuz oil transit.

Christopher Reed | Lead Business & Economy Investigative Reporter • May. 18, 2026
SHARE

TOKYO & LONDON — Global financial markets tumbled into risk-off territory on Monday while international benchmark crude oil prices surged past $84 per barrel, following an aggressive public warning from U.S. President Donald Trump declaring that "the clock is ticking" for the Iranian regime to reach a comprehensive diplomatic settlement or face devastating economic and military consequences. The escalating rhetoric heightened fears of imminent supply disruptions across the Persian Gulf, triggering selloffs in Asian and European stock bourses.

What Happened: Trump's White House Ultimatum Sparks Energy Spike

Addressing reporters during a high-stakes national security briefing, President Trump stated that diplomatic patience regarding Iran's uranium enrichment programs and missile deployments in the Red Sea has expired:

Energy markets reacted instantly to the prospect of open conflict. Brent crude futures surged 4.2% to trade at $84.60 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) climbed 4.5% to $80.25. Energy analysts warned that any disruption to the Strait of Hormuz—a narrow waterway that handles roughly 20% of global petroleum consumption—could propel oil prices above $100 within days.

Market Selloff: Asian and European Equities Retreat from Highs

Stock markets throughout the Asia-Pacific region retreated sharply from recent record valuations as energy cost anxieties dampened investor sentiment:

  • Japan & South Korea: Tokyo's Nikkei 225 fell 0.9% to 60,843, pressured by heavy losses in chipmakers and transport carriers, while Seoul's KOSPI shed 1.1% before recovering on late institutional buying.
  • Greater China Weakness: Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index plunged 1.6% and Shanghai Composite dropped following sluggish domestic retail sales figures that exacerbated global macro concerns.
  • Airlines & Consumer Stocks: Aviation shares, including ANA Holdings, Singapore Airlines, and Lufthansa, dropped between 3% and 5% due to immediate jet fuel price spikes.

Safe Haven Flight: Gold Surges as Dollar Gains Ground

As equity risk premiums expanded, institutional capital rushed into traditional sovereign safe-haven assets:

Spot gold prices surged $38 an ounce to hold firmly near historic records above $2,740, reflecting robust central bank and wealth management hedging. The U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) climbed to 104.3, advancing against the Japanese yen and British pound as investors sought liquid dollar cash reserves.

What to Watch Next: Naval Deployments and OPEC+ Production Policy

Market strategists and commodity traders are tracking three immediate flashpoints:

First is the posture of the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet in the Persian Gulf and whether tanker insurance war-risk premiums continue to escalate. Second, energy ministers from the OPEC+ alliance will evaluate whether to increase spare production quotas from Saudi Arabia and the UAE to counteract supply shocks. Third, Wall Street will watch whether high energy prices re-ignite headline inflation metrics, complicating Federal Reserve easing timelines.
