Measles Cases Top 5,400 as the US Edges Toward Losing Its Elimination Status

US measles cases reach 5,412 as a regional health body decides whether the country loses its elimination status, while HHS opens access to six peptides and funds ibogaine research.

A big week for American public health

It has been a heavy week for health news in the United States. Measles is spreading faster than it has in decades, and a major international health body is about to decide whether the country keeps a public health achievement it has held for more than 25 years. At the same time, federal health officials have opened the door to six unproven wellness peptides and put money behind a controversial psychedelic therapy for veterans. If you're trying to follow the latest health news in the US, here's a plain-English look at what's happening, why it matters for families, and what to watch next.

US Measles Cases Climb to 5,412

The latest CDC data, current as of November 12, shows 5,412 confirmed measles cases in 2026, about 2.4 times the 2,289 cases recorded in all of 2025. Health officials added 230 new cases in the past week, a 4.4% increase, which is slower than the 330 added the week before but still steady. Cases have now been reported in 47 jurisdictions, and the CDC counts 43 separate outbreaks, with 96% of cases linked to an outbreak. About 591 people, or 11% of all cases, have been hospitalized. The risk is highest for young children, with 17% of those under five needing hospital care. The CDC's tracker lists six measles deaths this year, though the Associated Press reports eight measles-associated deaths, so counts vary depending on the source. Around 96% of patients were either unvaccinated or had an unknown vaccination status.

Will the US Lose Its Measles Elimination Status?

The bigger question is what happens next. A commission of the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) is meeting this week to review genetic evidence on whether the same measles virus has been spreading continuously for a year or more in North America. If it has, the United States, which earned elimination status in 2000, would lose it, and so would Mexico. PAHO officials said an announcement is expected the following week, though no exact date has been given. Canada lost its status last year. Dr. David Margolius, director of Cleveland's health department, called it \"a big deal\" and said \"the decision represents us moving backwards.\" CDC Director Erica Schwartz offered a different view, saying, \"The measles elimination designation is not what protects Americans. Vaccination does.\" One reason for concern is that the national kindergarten MMR vaccination rate slipped to 92.4% in the 2025-2026 school year, below the 95% level experts say is needed to stop outbreaks.

HHS Opens the Door to Six Wellness Peptides

Another story getting attention is a new federal policy on peptides, which are short chains of amino acids popular with fitness influencers. Under a policy dated October 29, HHS is using what's called enforcement discretion, meaning the FDA won't take legal action against specialized compounding pharmacies that make and sell six peptides: BPC-157, Epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax and TB-500. The policy followed a July FDA advisory committee vote of 8 to 6 in favor of expanding access. Supporters, including HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., argue that licensed pharmacies are a safer source than online sellers. Critics disagree. Villanova law professor Ana Santos Rutschman, who studies FDA law, argues in Fortune that the policy doesn't make the peptides safer and leaves no regulator checking their safety or effectiveness. She also notes that FDA scientists had told the committee there wasn't enough information on the peptides' safety. If you're thinking about using any of them, talk to a doctor first

Federal Money Goes to Ibogaine Research for Veterans

On November 12, HHS announced up to $4.9 million over five years from the research agency ARPA-H to develop ibogaine, a psychedelic compound, as a possible treatment for PTSD, including in veterans. Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company is leading work on a domestically made ibogaine product, while Beond, Inc. will gather clinical data on its safety, dosing and effectiveness. It's important to be clear about what this does and doesn't do. The money funds groundwork for future trials. It doesn't pay for patient treatment or speed up FDA approval. Officials also acknowledge serious safety risks, including potentially life-threatening heart rhythm problems. Separately, the National Institute of Mental Health opened a $20 million funding opportunity for a research network studying ketamine, esketamine and psilocybin, with awards expected in summer 2027. Kennedy said, \"Veterans kept their promise to this country. We must keep ours.\"

Opioid Deaths Fall to Their Lowest Level Since 2017

There was some encouraging news too. During a recent \"Take Back Your Health Tour\" in Michigan and Iowa, Kennedy released an HHS analysis finding that opioid-related deaths have dropped to their lowest level since the 2017 public health emergency declaration. In Detroit, officials announced $12 million over four years for a program that connects people who are homeless and struggling with mental illness or addiction to treatment and housing. The tour also highlighted a pledge by more than 250 hospitals to serve healthier food, and the FDA announced proposed updates to infant formula nutrient requirements along with a new online dashboard for families. It's a reminder that, alongside the controversies, there are signs of progress on some of the country's toughest health problems.

What to Watch Next in US Health

So what should you keep an eye on? First, PAHO's decision on measles elimination, expected within days, and the CDC's next weekly update on case numbers. Second, whether the peptide policy draws legal challenges or new FDA guidance as more pharmacies begin selling these products. Third, which additional ibogaine and psychedelic research awards get announced in the coming months. For families, the most practical step is simple. Check that your children's vaccines are up to date, especially the MMR vaccine, and talk to your doctor before trying any new supplement or treatment you see online.
