Treasury Yields Top 5% as Stocks, Gold and Bitcoin Fall Together

The 10-year yield is above 5%, the dollar is near a 19-month high and gold, bitcoin and oil are under pressure as traders brace for Fed minutes and a December rate hike.

Nicole Patterson | Markets & Culture Contributor ' November 18, 2026

What to know

The 10-year Treasury yield has pushed back above 5% as traders bet on a December Federal Reserve rate hike. The dollar is near a 19-month high, while stocks, gold and bitcoin have fallen together and oil swings on attacks near the Strait of Hormuz.

When almost everything falls at once

Usually when stocks slide, investors run to safer places like gold or bonds. This week that hasn't happened. Stocks, gold and bitcoin have all dropped together, the dollar has climbed and Treasury yields have pushed back above 5%. A Korean business paper summed up the mood as \"everything falling\". At the heart of it is a simple worry: with inflation still stubborn, the Federal Reserve looks set to raise interest rates again next month, and that makes almost every other investment look less attractive. If you want to understand what's happening beyond the stock market, here's a plain-English look at bonds, the dollar, gold, oil and crypto, and why they matter for your money.

Treasury Yields Climb Back Above 5%

The bond market is where the pressure is most obvious. The 10-year Treasury yield has risen from about 4.86% on November 6 to above 5%, hitting around 5.14% last week and settling near 5.11% after Tuesday's close, while the 2-year yield was about 4.80%. A yield is basically the return investors demand for lending money to the government, and when it rises it pushes up borrowing costs for mortgages, car loans and business credit. The main trigger has been hotter-than-expected inflation data, which has led traders to rethink how aggressive the Fed will be. According to CME FedWatch, markets are pricing roughly a 95% to 96% chance of a quarter-point rate rise at the Fed's December 9 meeting. The minutes from October's meeting, due this afternoon, should show how many officials backed further tightening.

The Dollar Strengthens While Gold Slides

A rising yield tends to lift the dollar, and that is exactly what has happened. The dollar index climbed to around 103.6, close to a 19-month high, because higher US rates attract foreign money. That is bad news for gold, which pays no interest and gets more expensive for overseas buyers when the dollar rises. Gold fell from about $4,134 an ounce on November 9 to roughly $3,850 on Tuesday, a drop of more than 6% in about a week. Some banks still see a long-term case for the metal. Goldman Sachs, for example, projects gold could reach $4,650 by the end of 2027, partly because central banks keep buying. For now, though, the short-term pattern is clear: when real returns on cash and bonds rise, gold struggles.

Oil Prices Swing as Attacks Continue Around the Strait of Hormuz

Oil has been bouncing around as traders weigh war risks against rising supply. Brent crude settled at $93.14 a barrel on Tuesday, up 0.8%, after swinging in a range of about $3 during the session, while US West Texas Intermediate rose 0.3% to $86.01. Vessel attacks were reported in and around the Strait of Hormuz, Iran targeted Bahrain for the third time in less than a week, and a US military official said US warplanes struck an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island. Even so, prices have not surged, because supply is improving. Hormuz oil flows rose 78% in the third quarter to 9.1 million barrels a day, and the International Energy Agency forecasts a surplus of about 2 million barrels a day this quarter. Ryan McKay of TD Securities said, \"Crude oil prices should see some risk premium priced back into the market as tensions grow.\" For drivers there's some relief, since US gasoline prices have fallen to their lowest since March.

Bitcoin Struggles Around $64,000 as Money Leaves Crypto

Crypto has had an even rougher ride. Bitcoin was trading around $64,000 to $65,000 on Tuesday, roughly 16% below where it stood late last month, and it has struggled to reclaim $65,000. Ether slipped below $1,800, while Solana was one of the few coins to rise. Spot bitcoin ETFs saw net outflows of about $1.1 billion in the week to November 15, their third straight weekly withdrawal, and CoinDesk reported that money is leaving the market through stablecoins at the fastest pace since 2022. Analysts are pointing to the same things that are hurting stocks, namely high Treasury yields, weakness in equities and expectations of another Fed rate hike. If conditions worsen, some commentators warn that bitcoin could test $60,000, so it's a market where caution is the sensible approach.

Stocks Take a Hit, Then Nvidia Brings a Bounce

The stock market has been caught in the same storm. On Tuesday the Dow fell 848.75 points, or 1.64%, to 50,759.42, the S&P 500 dropped 1.03% to 7,340.53, its lowest close since late July, and the VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, rose to about 25.45. After the closing bell, Nvidia reported revenue of about $115.8 billion and guided to roughly $130 billion for the next quarter, and stock futures moved higher, with Nasdaq 100 futures up around 1.6% on Tuesday evening. Not everyone is convinced the bounce will last, because some early gains faded overnight. As Arne Lohmann Rasmussen of Global Risk Management put it when describing the oil market, it is \"a very nervous market\", and that description fits most of Wall Street right now.

What to Watch Next in the Markets

Several events could move prices over the next few days. First, the Fed minutes this afternoon, which will show how strongly officials favor more rate hikes. Second, whether Nvidia's after-hours gains hold when regular trading begins, since that will show if investors are regaining confidence in AI stocks. Third, reports that new US rules on AI chip access for companies in China are due to take effect on November 19, which China has signaled it could respond to. Fourth, Walmart's earnings on Thursday, which will give a fresh look at the American shopper. Finally, keep an eye on the Fed's December 9 decision. For everyday savers and borrowers, the message is that rates look likely to stay high for some time, so it makes sense to compare deals on mortgages and savings accounts rather than waiting for relief.
