DoorDash Forecast Beats Expectations Despite Rising Fuel Costs

DoorDash forecast stronger-than-expected order growth as delivery demand stayed resilient despite inflation and rising fuel costs tied to global oil disruptions.

Danielle Brooks | Senior Policy & US Markets Analyst • June. 17, 2026
SHARE

DoorDash forecast second-quarter marketplace gross order value above Wall Street expectations, signaling continued strength in delivery demand despite broader economic uncertainty and rising fuel costs. The company said it expects marketplace gross order value to range between $32.4 billion and $33.4 billion in the second quarter, above analyst estimates of around $31.8 billion.

DoorDash also reported better-than-expected adjusted profit and marketplace gross order value for the first quarter. Gross order value rose 37% year-over-year to $31.6 billion, while total orders climbed 27% to 933 million.

Revenue increased 33% to $4.04 billion, although it came in slightly below Wall Street expectations. Adjusted earnings reached 42 cents per share, beating analyst forecasts of 36 cents.

Investors reacted positively to the results, sending DoorDash shares up between 10% and 13% in after-hours trading. The company said strong demand for restaurant delivery, grocery services, retail expansion, and international growth helped support performance during the quarter.

Rising Fuel Costs and Driver Relief Program

DoorDash announced plans to spend more than $50 million during the second quarter on temporary gas price relief for delivery drivers in the United States and Canada. The program was introduced as fuel prices surged following the Iran war and disruptions in global oil markets.

The national average gasoline price in the U.S. recently reached $4.53 per gallon, up roughly 44% compared with a year earlier, according to AAA data cited in reports.

DoorDash had already spent approximately $50 million on similar gas support during the first quarter, meaning the company expects to allocate roughly $100 million toward fuel-related assistance during the first half of 2026.

The program included weekly relief payments tied to driving distance and fuel cashback incentives for drivers using the company’s Crimson debit card. Drivers traveling more miles received larger payments, with some eligible for up to $15 per week alongside 10% cashback on gas purchases.

Chief Financial Officer Ravi Inukonda said continuing the relief measures required the company to delay or scale back investments in other initiatives, though DoorDash maintained it remains committed to those long-term projects.

Expansion Beyond Restaurant Delivery

DoorDash’s growth continues to expand beyond traditional restaurant delivery services. The company said grocery, retail, apparel, and auto parts delivery contributed significantly to overall growth during the quarter.

Executives highlighted that customers increasingly rely on delivery services not only for meals but also for everyday essentials and convenience shopping. Reuters said that strong demand for convenience continues to support the delivery industry as a whole, even though inflation worries and rising consumer costs are a problem.

DoorDash is also expanding internationally by buying other companies and forming partnerships. Its purchase of Deliveroo has made it stronger in Europe and other places, and it still runs through subsidiaries like Wolt.

The company has also expanded into areas including grocery delivery, alcohol delivery, and multi-store ordering features such as DoubleDash.

CEO Tony Xu said DoorDash remains focused on growing its core restaurant and retail delivery business rather than aggressively diversifying into unrelated services.

Competition Across the Delivery Industry

DoorDash’s strong forecast comes as competitors across the delivery and grocery sector also reported resilient consumer demand. Reuters noted that both Instacart and Uber Eats recently posted stronger-than-expected delivery results despite inflation pressures and geopolitical uncertainty.

Instacart forecast second-quarter gross transaction value above Wall Street estimates as consumers continued prioritizing grocery and value-focused purchases. The company said spending patterns remained relatively stable despite economic pressure tied to inflation and supply chain disruptions.

At the same time, the delivery industry as a whole is still under pressure from rising costs of doing business, concerns about driver pay, and competition for customers. Many gig economy companies have started temporary fuel assistance programs because gas prices have gone up so much.

DoorDash is also still investing heavily in technology, automation, and logistics systems as competition heats up. Reports say that some planned investments, like robot delivery projects and system upgrades, may now be delayed because of the money spent on driver fuel relief. Despite these problems, analysts say that demand for convenience delivery services has stayed surprisingly strong even when the economy has been uncertain.

Financial Outlook and Long-Term Strategy

DoorDash maintained a positive long-term outlook despite rising costs and broader economic uncertainty. The company forecast adjusted EBITDA between $770 million and $870 million for the second quarter, above some analyst expectations.

The company’s adjusted profit performance helped ease investor concerns after DoorDash shares had fallen significantly earlier in the year following announcements about increased spending and technology investments.

Executives said DoorDash continues seeing strong engagement from both customers and merchants, supported by growing demand across restaurants, grocery stores, convenience retailers, and other delivery categories.

At the same time, the company acknowledged that fuel prices, geopolitical instability, and inflation remain major uncertainties that could affect both drivers and customers in coming months.

Still, DoorDash said it remains confident in the long-term strength of the delivery market and plans to continue investing in international growth, logistics technology, and broader retail partnerships while balancing near-term economic pressures.
