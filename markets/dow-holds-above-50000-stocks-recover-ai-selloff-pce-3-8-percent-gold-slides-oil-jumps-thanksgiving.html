Dow Holds 50,000 as Stocks Fight Back, Gold Slides and Oil Jumps

Stocks claw back most of a steep AI-led drop before Thanksgiving, hotter PCE inflation firms up December rate-hike bets, gold and silver slide, and oil jumps on a reported refinery attack.

A choppy week ends with a holiday pause

Wall Street has just closed out one of its bumpiest weeks of the year ahead of the Thanksgiving break. Tech and AI shares kept swinging, the Dow Jones Industrial Average stayed above 50,000, and a hotter inflation report made another Federal Reserve rate hike look nearly certain. Elsewhere, gold and silver fell sharply and oil jumped. US markets are shut today for Thanksgiving and will close early at 1:00 p.m. Eastern on Friday. Here is a plain-English recap of the latest US markets news.

Tuesday: Tech Slides While the Dow Climbs

On Tuesday, November 24, the Dow rose 0.85%, or 423 points, to 50,018.70, helped by health care and consumer stocks. The S&P 500 slipped 0.50% to 7,201.09, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.48% to 23,733.54, as chip and AI shares gave back some of Monday's big rebound. Nvidia fell about 5.9% and Micron about 11%. Health care was the best sector after Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed for $41 billion in cash. Retailers also did well, with Abercrombie & Fitch, Kohl's and Dick's Sporting Goods jumping after strong results, and consumer confidence rose to 103.3, well above forecasts. New York Fed President John Williams also backed a December rate hike.

Wednesday: Stocks Recover Most of a Steep Early Drop

Wednesday, November 25, started badly. The S&P 500 fell as much as 3.2% and the Nasdaq as much as 4.6% in the morning, and the Dow was down more than 1,100 points at one stage. By the close, most of the damage had been undone. The Dow ended up 0.33% at 50,182.95, the S&P 500 finished down 0.25% at 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq slipped 0.62% to 23,585.80. Mid-sized and small companies did best, with the S&P MidCap 400 up 1.23% and the Russell 2000 up 0.71%. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, rose to about 28.3 after topping 30 in the morning. Among individual stocks, HP Inc. jumped about 11.5% after beating forecasts, Deere rose 4.4%, and Nvidia fell another 3.9%.

Hotter Inflation Locks In December Rate-Hike Bets

The day's main economic reports showed a strong economy and stubborn prices. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the PCE price index, rose 3.8% from a year earlier in October, with core PCE up 3.3%, slightly above forecasts. The second estimate of third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualized. Futures markets now price roughly a 93% to 98% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike at the December 8 to 9 meeting, depending on the source. In the bond market, the 10-year Treasury yield fell about 6 basis points to around 4.85%, as investors bought bonds during the stock swings.

Gold and Silver Slide While Oil Jumps

Commodities were volatile. Gold fell about 0.7% on Wednesday to roughly $3,535 an ounce, after dipping below $3,500 during the day, and reports show it has dropped sharply over the past month as rate-hike expectations rise and the dollar firms. Silver fell even more. Oil moved the other way. Brent crude rose about 3.5% to about $94.61 a barrel, and US crude rose about 2% to $83.68, according to one market recap, which linked the move to a reported Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery. That report should be confirmed with a second source. Bitcoin held around $60,000.

What to Watch Next

Keep an eye on three things. First, Friday's shortened session, when US markets close at 1:00 p.m. Eastern and thin trading can exaggerate moves. Second, Nvidia, which has fallen for several days even after Huang's return, and whether chip stocks stabilise. Third, the Fed's December 8 to 9 meeting and any more official comments, since a hike is already expected. Check the latest prices before you publish.
